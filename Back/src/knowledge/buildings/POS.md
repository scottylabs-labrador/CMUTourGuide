# Posner Hall

## Overview

Posner Hall is adjacent to the Hall of the Arts, which is home to some teaching and rehearsal spaces for the School of Music. This building is home to a student-favorite eatery called The Exchange as well as the Student Academic Success Center. The  Student Academic Success Center focuses on creating spaces for students to engage in their coursework and approach to learning through options like academic coaching, subject-specific tutoring, effective communication strategies, accommodations for students with disabilities, and language support for multilingual learners.

Built 1990–1993 as an expansion of the Graduate School of Industrial Administration by Kallmann McKinnell & Wood, Posner Hall was the original home of the Tepper School of Business until Tepper relocated to Tepper Quad in 2018. A full 2021 GBBN renovation (LEED Gold) repurposed the building for the Student Academic Success Center (1st floor), registrar classrooms, Dietrich College departments, and the Institute for Politics and Strategy. A gift of Henry Posner Jr. and his family. The star attraction is Kraus Campo (2005) — a rooftop garden-as-sculpture designed by conceptual artist Mel Bochner (CFA '62) — who died February 12, 2025 at 84 — with landscape architect Michael Van Valkenburgh, commissioned by trustee Jill Gansman Kraus (A'74). Features include a central 25-by-60-foot tile-covered platform shaped like a French curve and patterned with embedded black numbers in white tiles — a hallmark of Bochner's lifelong interest in numerical sequencing — winding orange pathways through plantings, and a 6-foot-by-58-foot tiled quotation — words referencing physicist Arthur Eddington on the second law of thermodynamics, attributed in context to Ludwig Wittgenstein, transcribed in reverse order as a literal visual puzzle that students walk past daily without ever quite finishing. Dining is The Exchange on the first floor — a 20-year CMU institution serving custom deli sandwiches (student favorite: "The Leonardo"), made-from-scratch soups, hot entrées, a salad bar, and La Prima coffee, sourced from local BreadWorks and Turner's Dairy Farm. The adjacent earth-sheltered Posner Center (2004, 11,400 sq ft) houses the Posner Memorial Collection of rare books and early calculating machines. In March 2025, the Posner Foundation committed $8 million to CMU Libraries Special Collections, funding a Perkins Eastman-designed renovation of the Posner Center scheduled to conclude fall 2025, reimagining it as "part museum, part laboratory for the humanities."
