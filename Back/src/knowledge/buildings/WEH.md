# Wean Hall

## Overview

Opened 1971, renamed October 9, 1981 for industrialist Raymond "Jack" Wean (Carnegie Tech 1917), designed by Dahlen K. Ritchey for $15.5 million (1971 dollars). Wean is CMU's sole brutalist building — a massive reinforced-concrete bunker 8 stories tall, built into the side of a ravine so that only 3 stories plus a mechanical penthouse are visible from the Mall side, the remaining 5 descending into the hillside. No two floors have identical layouts. Nicknamed "The Turtle." It was Pittsburgh's largest all-concrete building after Three Rivers Stadium was imploded in 2001. Houses the Department of Mathematical Sciences, parts of the School of Computer Science (classrooms, labs, clusters), Physics labs, the Sorrells Engineering and Science Library, and Machine Learning Department offices on the subterranean level. Wean connects to Doherty through a warren of stairs and hallways at five different levels — the site of the student game "Capture the Flag with Stuff." Major facade repairs in 2013–2014 exposed and encased rusted rebar ahead of the SCS 50th anniversary. A recent lobby renovation by IKM Architecture added a glass "crystal" prism under the brutalist overhang to create a light-filled, recruitment-friendly entrance housing an expanded La Prima Espresso. Dining is La Prima Espresso Company — a staple for 30+ years, Pittsburgh's only certified organic coffee roaster, serving Italian-style espresso, nitro cold brew, Mediterra Bakehouse pastries, and curried chickpea wraps. Fun fact: students claim an unofficial world record for "most bottle caps stuck in holes in a building" — plastic bottle caps are the exact size of the form-tie holes in Wean's concrete walls. Scott Fahlman, whose office sat in the CS computing ecosystem, invented the :-) emoticon at CMU on September 19, 1982 at 11:44 AM, in a post on a CMU bulletin board proposing it as a "joke marker." The Pittsburgh Supercomputing Center (founded 1986 by CMU physicist Michael Levine) is closely affiliated with Wean's computing history.
