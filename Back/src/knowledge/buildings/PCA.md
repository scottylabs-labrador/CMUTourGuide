# Purnell Center for the Arts

## Overview

Purnell Center for the Arts is home to our School of Drama, the nation’s oldest degree-granting drama program, and includes state-of-the-art performance and education facilities like the Philip Chosky Theater and the Regina Gouger Miller Gallery. Whether you see yourself on stage, behind the scenes, or forging a creative path all on your own, our School of Drama’s specialized curriculum and dedicated, world-class faculty can help you get there with majors in acting/musical theater, design, dramaturgy, and production technology & management.

Opened in 2000 and named for alumnus-donor Verner S. Purnell, Purnell is home to the School of Drama — the oldest degree-granting drama program in the United States (founded 1914) — plus the Miller Institute for Contemporary Art. Approximately 110,000 sq ft, designed by the DDF Associates joint venture (Michael Dennis and Associates with theatre specialist John Sergio Fisher AIA, plus local firm Damianos + Anthony) in a neoclassical style with a rotunda lobby mirroring Margaret Morrison's across campus. Admit rate for the drama program is under 3% in recent years — the lowest at CMU. Philip Chosky Theater is the ~500-seat proscenium mainstage (convertible to thrust), featuring a 24′×18′ trap door section, tension grid, motorized orchestra pit, and computerized rigging — it replaced the 90-year-old Kresge Theatre. Helen Wayne Rauh Studio Theatre is the 130–140-seat flexible black-box. The building also houses the John Wells Video Studio (three-camera TV sound stage), a world-renowned lighting lab, and two dance studios. Miller ICA is a free, three-story, 8,000-sq-ft contemporary gallery founded in 2000 by alumna Regina Gouger Miller; it hosts the annual MFA Exhibition and BFA/BXA Senior Shows. The signature student tradition is "Playground: A Festival of Independent Student Work," running for 20+ years — the School of Drama cancels all classes for the week while students propose, produce, and perform 60+ original works across three days, spanning new plays, musical performances, lighting and art installations, murals, and multimedia projects. Notable Playground alumni include Patina Miller (Broadway's "Sister Act"), Josh Gad ("The Book of Mormon"), and Amanda Jane Cooper ("Wicked" national tour); the PigPen Theatre Company first performed together at Playground in 2007 before going on to win Best of the New York International Fringe Festival and compose the music for Broadway's "Water for Elephants." The alumni roll is legendary: Ted Danson, Holly Hunter, Zachary Quinto, Josh Groban, Patrick Wilson, Steven Bochco, Ethan Hawke, Sutton Foster, Megan Hilty, Cherry Jones, Judith Light, Rob Marshall, Billy Porter, Leslie Odom Jr., Renée Elise Goldsberry, Christian Borle. CMU alumni have won numerous major industry honors including 66 Tony Awards, 99+ Emmys, and 13 Academy Awards. On April 28, 2014 — coinciding with the School of Drama's centennial — CMU became the first and exclusive higher-education partner of the Tony Awards, co-creating the "Tony Honor for Excellence in Theatre Education presented by Carnegie Mellon University," the first national recognition program for K–12 theatre educators (introduced on the June 8, 2014 broadcast by CMU alumni Zachary Quinto and Matt Bomer; first recipient honored at the 2015 Tony Awards).
