# Hamerschlag Hall

## Overview

Hamerschlag Hall is home to Electrical & Computer Engineering. The building's rotunda is one of the most iconic images on campus. This building also boasts a living roof

Hornbostel's 1912 Machinery Hall — renamed in 1964 for Arthur A. Hamerschlag, first president of Carnegie Technical Schools (1903–1922) — is home to the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering (450 undergrads, 300 grads) plus Mechanical Engineering labs and College of Engineering classrooms. The iconic feature is the domed round tower, a functional ~64-foot smokestack disguising the old power plant's flue and often called "the most beautiful smokestack ever created." Hornbostel designed the building as "the helm of an explorer's ship" pointing toward the Carnegie Institute, with a perfect axial view to Pitt's Cathedral of Learning. The entrance features a copy of the U.S.S. Pittsburgh cruiser's bow ornament. The cream-colored Kittanning brick was Hornbostel's deliberate signal of "practical, modern" identity versus red-brick Ivy League schools. Inside sits the HH-1107 Undergraduate Computer Cluster (recently upgraded as a makerspace), the Additive Manufacturing Lab, and the historic cleanroom facilities — though most nanofabrication has migrated to the Eden Hall Foundation Cleanroom — roughly 8,500 sq ft of Class 10/100 cleanroom inside the larger Bertucci Nanotechnology Laboratory in the adjacent Sherman and Joyce Bowie Scott Hall (which opened April 30, 2016, with the cleanroom fully operational in 2017). In 2016, Volpatt Construction replaced 68 historic wood single-pane windows with custom-matched profiles. The tower is CMU's single most recognizable visual icon, appearing on nearly every postcard and promotional image. (Don't confuse Hamerschlag Hall with Hamerschlag House, a separate first-year residence nicknamed "Schlag" that was renovated in 2024–2025.)
