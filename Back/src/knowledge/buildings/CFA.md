# College of Fine Arts

## Overview

College of Fine Arts Building is an architectural creation of Henry Hornbostel, the college’s first dean, and is home to three of its five schools: architecture, art, and music.

It includes the school of Music, Art, and Architecture

The CFA building is Henry Hornbostel's tribute to his own education at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris — cornerstone laid April 25, 1912, building completed in 1917. The College of Fine Arts itself was founded in 1905 as one of the first comprehensive fine-arts colleges in the United States; its five schools — Architecture, Art, Design, Drama, and Music — were formally consolidated under that structure by 1995. The cream-brick Beaux-Arts façade features five exterior niches with reliefs representing Greek, Roman, Medieval, Renaissance, and non-Western stylistic periods, but only the Renaissance niche was carved when the building opened in 1917. The other four sat empty for nearly 70 years, until alumnus Verner S. Purnell (CMU 1926) funded their completion in 1987; carving was executed 1990–1993 by an international team of stone masons led by Nicholas Fairplay, Bruce Lindsey, and Paul Rosenblatt and described at the time as "the most important stonework project underway in North America." In the Roman niche above the entrance, a bust of Bacchus is carved with Hornbostel's own face, paired with a bust of Mercury depicting Verner S. Purnell himself. The Great Hall's marble floors display embedded plans of the world's great historical monuments so students literally walk over architectural history; the vaulted ceiling murals — painted by James Hewlett — include a depiction of Hornbostel's own Hell Gate Bridge in New York. Kresge Theatre (255 seats) is the recital and convocation venue; Alumni Concert Hall serves large ensembles. No dining inside, but Ginger's Express in Purnell and The Exchange in Posner are steps away. The alumni list is the tour-app headliner. School of Art: Andy Warhol (Pictorial Design, 1949), Philip Pearlstein, Mel Bochner, John Currin, Jonathan Borofsky. School of Drama (founded 1914, the oldest degree-granting drama program in the U.S., admit rate under 3% in recent years versus ~18% for the College of Fine Arts overall): Ted Danson, Holly Hunter, Ethan Hawke, Jeff Goldblum, Zachary Quinto, Patrick Wilson, Matt Bomer, Josh Gad, Josh Groban, Leslie Odom Jr., Billy Porter, Judith Light, Christian Borle, Rob Marshall, Steven Bochco. Music: Stephen Schwartz (composer of Wicked), Keith Lockhart (Boston Pops). Hornbostel himself wore red satin string ties, kept a pet monkey, and drove a convertible with his dog as co-pilot.
