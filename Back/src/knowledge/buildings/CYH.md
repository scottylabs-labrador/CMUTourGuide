# Cyert Hall

## Overview

president Originally the University Computing Center, renamed for Richard Michael Cyert (1921–1998), CMU's 6th president from 1972–1990 and co-author of the seminal 1959 A Behavioral Theory of the Firm. Cyert's presidency transformed CMU "from an Eastern technical school to a premier American university," launching the largest expansion in school history and focusing the university on computing. The building is a workmanlike concrete mid/late-20th-century academic structure at 5000 Forbes Avenue. It houses CMU Computing Services — the university's main network infrastructure and ITS data center (rack-mount colocation, web servers, AFS/DFS/NFS storage, authentication systems); the Office of International Education (Suite 101 — study abroad advising, F-1/J-1 visas, I-20/DS-2019 issuance); the Osher Lifelong Learning Institute (in a 2021 renovation of 5,730 sq ft by NEXT Architecture); Desk Services tech help (Room 119); and student computer labs. The CS Department itself was established in 1965 with a $5M Mellon grant — Alan Perlis (the very first ACM Turing Award recipient in 1966) was its first head. Cyert Hall is effectively the plumbing of CMU's digital life — the first stop for international students handling visas and for students launching study-abroad journeys.
