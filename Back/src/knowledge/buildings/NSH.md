# Newell-Simon Hall

## Overview

Newell-Simon Hall (NSH) is a six-story academic building located in the northwestern part of Carnegie Mellon's campus, adjacent to Wean Hall (which connects to NSH via a skyway bridge on Wean's 4th floor).

The building is named after Herbert A. Simon and Allen Newell, two CMU professors who were pioneers of artificial intelligence and cognitive science. Simon and Newell shared the 1975 ACM Turing Award for their contributions to AI and the psychology of human cognition. Simon additionally won the 1978 Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences for his pioneering research on decision-making within economic organizations. Together they developed some of the earliest AI programs at CMU in the late 1950s, including the Logic Theorist (often considered the first artificial intelligence program) and the General Problem Solver.

NSH was originally constructed in 1934 and was built atop two earlier buildings (Buildings C and D) acquired from the United States Bureau of Mines, which had a major Pittsburgh experiment station on the site.

The building houses three of the School of Computer Science's flagship units: the Robotics Institute, the Human-Computer Interaction Institute (HCII), and the Quality of Life Technology Center.

The Robotics Institute, founded in October 1979 by President Dick Cyert, Raj Reddy, and Angel Jordan with support from Westinghouse Corporation, was the first academic department in the world devoted exclusively to robotics. Raj Reddy served as its founding director from 1979 to 1991, and the Institute introduced the world's first Ph.D. program in robotics in 1988.

A major renovation and addition project for the Robotics Institute portion of NSH was designed by NEXT architecture and completed at a cost of approximately $5.8 million. The project added 25,000 square feet of renovated teaching labs and collaboration space, plus a cantilevered conference room overlooking the Robotics Institute high bay. It achieved LEED Silver certification with 80% of construction waste diverted from landfills. Notable renovation challenges included an uneven first floor that required slab leveling, extensive mechanical upgrades, and a mysterious water reservoir discovered behind a first-floor wall.

About the namesakes: Allen Newell (1927–1992) was a CMU computer science and cognitive psychology professor, recipient of the 1975 Turing Award (with Simon) and the 1992 IEEE Emanuel R. Piore Award. Herbert A. Simon (1916–2001) was a CMU professor across multiple disciplines (Computer Science, Psychology, Economics, Public Policy), recipient of the 1975 Turing Award and the 1978 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences, and is considered one of the founders of the fields of artificial intelligence, cognitive psychology, and behavioral economics.
