# Margaret Morrison Carnegie Hall

## Overview

Margaret Morrison Carnegie Hall, affectionately known on campus as 'Maggie Mo'. This building originally housed the Margaret Morrison Carnegie College for Women, and it now houses some years of the design and architecture programs as well as our on campus children’s school!

The School of Design is an exploration of humanity and sustainable systems. With a focus on interdisciplinary learning, our programs prepare students for real-world design challenges by working collaboratively across fields. CMU was the first school to offer a degree in Industrial Design. Now it’s called “Product Design”.

Built 1906– 1907 by Henry Hornbostel as the Margaret Morrison Carnegie School for Women — CMU's original women's college, named for Andrew Carnegie's mother — this is now the home of the School of Design (CFA) plus parts of the School of Architecture, including the renowned Center for Building Performance and Diagnostics. Alumnae were called "Maggie Murphs." The women's college closed in 1973 due to declining enrollment as CMU fully integrated. The signature feature is the grand semicircular colonnaded rotunda with inscribed dedication from 1907 (you'll see references to both Ionic and Doric column orders across sources — the SAH Archipedia says Doric). Inside the rotunda, alumna Rebecca Deutsch ('04) installed stenciled quotes from women students across CMU history, responding to the original inscription. The rooftop hosts the Robert L. Preger Intelligent Workplace — a 7,000-sq-ft shimmering glass penthouse added in 1997 as an internationally recognized green-building research lab that predates LEED certification. A 1990–1996 Bohlin Cywinski Jackson renovation updated the interior. Hornbostel originally intended a matching rear wing with a second rotunda; it was never built. Notable alumna: Stephanie Kwolek (MMCC '46) — inventor of Kevlar.
