# Hamburg Hall

## Overview

Built 1915–1917 by Henry Hornbostel as the main building of the U.S. Bureau of Mines Central Experiment Station, Hamburg was acquired by CMU in 1984–1985 (U.S. Senator H. John Heinz III was instrumental) and is on the National Register of Historic Places since 1974. Today it's the headquarters of Heinz College — CMU's dual School of Public Policy & Management and School of Information Systems & Management. A 2013–2017, $18M+ renovation by GBBN Architects, funded by a $10M gift from The Heinz Endowments, inserted a 150-seat glass-enclosed auditorium into the former courtyard between Hamburg and Smith Halls, achieving LEED Gold for that phase. The Hamburg Hall 1000 rotunda was reimagined as a grand lobby, and a 3,600-sq-ft rooftop plaza sits above the new auditorium.

Heinz College has ~1,500 students and operated a branch campus in Adelaide, Australia from 2006 until it was wound down following a June 2022 announcement (over 1,200 students completed degrees through the Adelaide campus during its 16-year run). Weekly speakers have included Senator Bob Casey, former San Juan Mayor Carmen Yulín Cruz (MSPPM '86), Martin O'Malley, MoMA Director Glenn Lowry, and author Ijeoma Oluo. The school was renamed for Senator Heinz in 1992 following his 1991 death, with a lead gift from Teresa Heinz. Current Dean: Kirsten Martin (the H. John Heinz III Dean of the Heinz College of Information Systems and Public Policy; her research focuses on technology ethics and algorithmic accountability). Notable faculty include Alessandro Acquisti (privacy economics) and Jonathan Caulkins (drug policy). Alumna Leah Lizarondo founded Pittsburgh's 412 Food Rescue.
