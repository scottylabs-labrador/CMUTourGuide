# Gesling Stadium

## Overview

Gesling Stadium (commonly nicknamed 'The G') is a 3,900-seat stadium located on Carnegie Mellon's campus at 5000 Forbes Avenue, Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. It opened in 1990 as part of CMU's east campus project, which also included two new residence halls and a 700-space parking garage. CMU's official Athletics page lists the seating as 3,500 in the grandstands plus additional standing-room viewing.

The field at Gesling Stadium is officially dedicated to longtime Carnegie Mellon football head coach Rich Lackner, making the full name 'Richard M. Lackner Field at Gesling Stadium.' The naming was announced May 29, 2024 by the CMU Athletics Department in recognition of Lackner's coaching legacy.

The stadium is the home venue for the Carnegie Mellon Tartans varsity football team (fall season) and the men's and women's track and field teams (spring season). It also hosts club and intramural sports, including Rugby and Quidditch (Quadball). From 1990 to 2010 it additionally served as the home of CMU's varsity men's and women's soccer teams.

The playing surface is FieldTurf, originally installed in summer 2006 and most recently resurfaced in summer 2025. The infill mix uses silica sand and cryogenic rubber designed to emulate natural grass. The 8-lane running track encircling the field was resurfaced in summer 2016.

A state-of-the-art 13-foot by 24-foot video scoreboard was installed in 2019. The Jared L. Cohon University Center (CUC) forms the backdrop to the stadium's west end zone, while the Judith Resnik and West Wing residence halls overshadow the away sideline. Nearby dining facilities include the Carnegie Mellon Cafe (2006) and Tartans Pavilion (2007).

Despite Carnegie Mellon competing at the NCAA Division III level (in the Presidents' Athletic Conference), Gesling Stadium is notable for being the largest stadium in the city of Pittsburgh purpose-built primarily for college football. The University of Pittsburgh's Panthers (Division I) play at Acrisure Stadium, which was primarily built for the NFL Pittsburgh Steelers; the Duquesne Dukes' Arthur J. Rooney Athletic Field has a capacity of only 2,200.
