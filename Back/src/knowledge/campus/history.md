# History of Carnegie Mellon University

## Founding

Carnegie Mellon was founded in 1900 by Andrew Carnegie, the Scottish-born steel industrialist who emigrated to the United States in 1848 and settled in Pittsburgh. He donated $1 million to establish the Carnegie Technical Schools, intended to give the working-class sons and daughters of Pittsburgh a practical education. The first class was admitted in 1906.

## Carnegie Tech years

In 1912 the Carnegie Technical Schools became the Carnegie Institute of Technology, known as Carnegie Tech, and began awarding bachelor's degrees. The first drama degree in the United States was awarded at Carnegie Tech in 1914. The first doctorate, in civil engineering, went to Mao Yisheng of China in 1919, the same year Andrew Carnegie died.

In 1938 the school adopted the Carnegie Plan, which required science and engineering students to take humanities and social science courses, an early version of the interdisciplinary approach CMU is known for today. The Graduate School of Industrial Administration was established in 1948 and later became the Tepper School of Business. The first IBM computer arrived on campus in 1956.

## Becoming Carnegie Mellon

In 1967 Carnegie Tech merged with the Mellon Institute of Industrial Research, founded by the Mellon banking family, to form Carnegie Mellon University. The School of Urban and Public Affairs, founded in 1968, later became Heinz College. Margaret Morrison Carnegie College, the original women's college, closed in 1973 and women were fully integrated into the main campus. The School of Computer Science was established in 1986.

## Global campuses

CMU opened a branch campus in Silicon Valley in 2002 and Carnegie Mellon University in Qatar in 2004, which began with 41 students. A partnership with the government of Rwanda established CMU-Africa in 2011.
