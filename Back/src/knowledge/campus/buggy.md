# Buggy (Sweepstakes)

## What it is

Buggy, officially called Sweepstakes, is part high-tech soapbox derby and part relay race, and it is unique to Carnegie Mellon. Teams of five athletes called pushers propel a small, low, aerodynamic, unmotorized vehicle, the buggy, around a roughly 0.8 mile course through Schenley Park, using the buggy itself as the relay baton. A driver lies inside the buggy and steers it through the downhill freeroll section at speeds up to about 35 miles per hour. Roughly 12 to 15 student organizations race each year, about half of them Greek organizations, and student mechanics design and build new buggies from raw materials.

## The course and the race

The course starts near Margaret Morrison Carnegie Hall at Tech Street, loops around Flagstaff Hill in Schenley Park, and finishes at Tech Street and Frew Street behind campus. Races are held on Friday and Saturday mornings of Spring Carnival: preliminary heats Friday morning, exhibition heats early Saturday, and the finals Saturday morning. Teams practice on weekend mornings in the weeks before Carnival, which is why early risers see buggies rolling through the park in the spring.

## History

The first race was announced in The Tartan in April 1920 as the Interfraternity Motor Sweepstakes and run during Campus Week that spring, with ten fraternities entering. A witness described the first entries as a conglomeration of rain barrels with bicycle wheels, orange crates, and ash cans. The original course started outside what is now the College of Fine Arts building, ran up Tech Street into Schenley Park, and back to campus, with pit stops to swap rear wheels. Racing paused during World War II from 1943 to 1945 and resumed in 1946 with nine teams. The first documented female driver, Mary K. Skriba, raced in 1973, and official women's heats were added in 1979. In recent years the School of Computer Science has trialed an autonomous buggy named Singularity, which navigated most of the course in a test run. Buggy has been held on or behind campus every year since 1920, and celebrated its centennial in 2020.
